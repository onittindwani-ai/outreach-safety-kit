# Sources

Every rule about LinkedIn in this kit quotes or links one of LinkedIn's own pages below. All pages were read on 2026-11-09. LinkedIn can change any of them at any time, so open the page itself before you rely on a line here.

Labels:

- **Primary** means the organisation's own page or the text of the law.
- **Secondary** means a news report. Treat what it says as what the reporter was told.
- **Self reported** means a company describing its own case. Nobody independent has confirmed it.

The lines starting with `>` are exact quotes. `tests/check_kit.py` fails if a quote used in a check is not on this page.

## LinkedIn's own pages (Primary)

### LinkedIn User Agreement

https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement (page says "Effective on November 3, 2025", read 2026-11-09)

Section 2.1, what you promise when you use LinkedIn:

> you will only have one LinkedIn account, which must be in your real name; and (3) you are not already restricted by LinkedIn from using the Services.

Section 2.2, your account:

> not share or transfer your account or any part of it

Section 3.4, limits:

> LinkedIn reserves the right to restrict, suspend, or terminate your account if you breach this Contract or the law or are misusing the Services

Section 8.1, Dos:

> Comply with all applicable laws, including, without limitation, privacy laws, intellectual property laws, anti-spam laws, export control laws, laws governing the content shared, and other applicable laws and regulatory requirements;

Section 8.2, Don'ts:

> Create a false identity on LinkedIn, misrepresent your identity, create a Member profile for anyone other than yourself (a real person), or use or attempt to use another’s account (such as sharing log-in credentials or copying cookies);

> Develop, support or use software, devices, scripts, robots or any other means or processes (such as crawlers, browser plugins and add-ons or any other technology) to scrape or copy the Services, including profiles and other data from the Services;

> Override any security feature or bypass or circumvent any access controls or use limits of the Services (such as search results, profiles, or videos);

> Copy, use, display or distribute any information (including content) obtained from the Services, whether directly or through third parties (such as search tools or data aggregators or brokers), without the consent of the content owner (such as LinkedIn for content it owns);

> using the word “LinkedIn” or our logos in any business name, email, or URL except as provided in the Brand Guidelines;

> Imply or state that you are affiliated with or endorsed by LinkedIn without our express consent (e.g., representing yourself as an accredited LinkedIn trainer);

> Rent, lease, loan, trade, sell/re-sell or otherwise monetize the Services or related data or access to the same, without LinkedIn’s consent;

> Deep-link to our Services for any purpose other than to promote your profile or a Group on our Services, without LinkedIn’s consent;

> Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages, create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts, or otherwise drive inauthentic engagement;

> Engage in “framing”, “mirroring”, or otherwise simulating the appearance or function of the Services;

> Misuse our reporting or appeals process, including by submitting duplicative, fraudulent or unfounded reports, complaints or appeals.

## The Artisan case (Secondary)

### TechCrunch, "Yes, LinkedIn banned AI agent startup Artisan, but now it's back"

https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/07/yes-linkedin-banned-ai-agent-startup-artisan-but-now-its-back/ (by Julie Bort, 7 January 2026, read 2026-11-09)

Every reason below is the account Artisan's CEO, Jaspar Carmichael-Jack, gave to TechCrunch. LinkedIn did not confirm them in that report.

> LinkedIn did, however, object to the startup using LinkedIn’s name on its website and also alleged that the company was using data brokers who had scraped the site without permission, Carmichael-Jack said.

> It was using the name to compare some of its data features to LinkedIn’s.

> To appease LinkedIn, Artisan removed all mentions of LinkedIn from its website.

> The CEO also got a crash course in third-party vendor verification, ensuring that his data partners were operating in compliance with LinkedIn’s policies.

> LinkedIn did not immediately respond to TechCrunch’s request for comment.

Also from this report: the email from LinkedIn arrived on Friday evening, December 19; the page, employee profiles and executive posts showed a "This post cannot be displayed" message; Artisan was being reinstated after working with LinkedIn over about two weeks; the team was reachable only by email.

## Law and regulators (Primary)

### FTC, CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business

https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business (read 2026-11-09)

> The law makes no exception for business-to-business email.

> You must honor a recipient’s opt-out request within 10 business days.

> Once people have told you they don’t want to receive more messages from you, you can’t sell or transfer their email addresses, even in the form of a mailing list.

> you can’t contract away your legal responsibility to comply with the law.

### GDPR Article 14, data not obtained from the person

https://gdpr-info.eu/art-14-gdpr/ (read 2026-11-09, unofficial reprint of the regulation text)

> from which source the personal data originate, and if applicable, whether it came from publicly accessible sources;

> if the personal data are to be used for communication with the data subject, at the latest at the time of the first communication to that data subject;

### GDPR Article 21, right to object

https://gdpr-info.eu/art-21-gdpr/ (read 2026-11-09, unofficial reprint of the regulation text)

> Where the data subject objects to processing for direct marketing purposes, the personal data shall no longer be processed for such purposes.

### California Privacy Protection Agency, information for data brokers

https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers/ (read 2026-11-09)

> To fall under the definition of data broker, a business must knowingly collect and sell to “third parties the personal information of a consumer with whom the business does not have a direct relationship.”

> beginning August 1, 2026, data brokers must access the “accessible deletion mechanism” at least once every 45 days and process consumer deletion requests

The same page links the current data broker registry.

## What LinkedIn does not publish

On the pages read for this kit, LinkedIn does not publish a list of approved outreach tools or a daily or weekly number that is safe to send. The invitation pages say limits exist and do not give the number. The commercial use limit page says LinkedIn cannot display how many searches or views you have left. Numbers you see elsewhere come from vendors and bloggers. This kit repeats none of them.
