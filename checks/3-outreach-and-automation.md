# Group 3: Your outreach and automation (checks 21 to 30)

Artisan's CEO said the ban was not about AI spam, but that does not make automation fine. LinkedIn's rules against automated outreach are written plainly, and many AI SDR products depend on exactly that.

This kit gives no safe daily number. LinkedIn does not publish one, and on the pages read for this kit it says it cannot show you what you have left. Any number you see elsewhere comes from a vendor or a blogger.

## Check 21: A list of every tool that touches LinkedIn

**Priority:** Fix first
**Based on:** LinkedIn rule

### What to look at

Every browser extension on every sales laptop, every outreach tool, every AI agent or script that opens LinkedIn, and every cloud service you have given a LinkedIn login to.

### How to check it

Have each person open the extensions page of their browser and list what is installed. Check your software bills and the tools connected to your accounts. Write one row per tool: what it does on LinkedIn, and who uses it.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** you have a complete written list, and every tool on it passes checks 22 to 24.
- **Fail:** there is no list, or it contains a tool that scrapes, changes how LinkedIn looks or automates activity.

### The fix

Remove what fails. Note that LinkedIn publishes no list of approved outreach tools on the pages read for this kit, so a tool's own claim to be safe tells you little.

### Source

- [LinkedIn Help, prohibited software and extensions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387):
  > we don't permit the use of any third party software, including "crawlers", bots, browser plug-ins, or browser extensions that scrape, modify the appearance of, or automate activity on LinkedIn's website.
  > Any member who uses tools for such purposes is in violation of the User Agreement. This means that they risk having their accounts restricted or shut down.

## Check 22: Automated invitations and messages

**Priority:** Fix first
**Based on:** LinkedIn rule

### What to look at

Whether any invitation or message on LinkedIn is sent by software instead of a person clicking send, including AI agents, sequencers and scheduled sends.

### How to check it

For each tool on your list, ask: who presses send? If the answer is the software, this check fails. Read the tool's setup page to see whether it needs your LinkedIn session.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** a real person sends every invitation and message from inside LinkedIn, or through something LinkedIn has authorised in writing.
- **Fail:** any software sends them for you.

### The fix

Stop the automation. Let your AI write drafts in your own system, then have a person read, edit and send each one by hand.

### Source

- [LinkedIn User Agreement, section 8.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement):
  > Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages, create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts, or otherwise drive inauthentic engagement;

## Check 23: Automated likes, comments and engagement swaps

**Priority:** Fix first
**Based on:** LinkedIn rule

### What to look at

Auto likes, auto comments, AI comment tools that post for you, scheduled engagement, and pods where people agree to like each other's posts.

### How to check it

List any tool or group that creates engagement on your posts or your team's posts. Check your team's group chats for agreements to engage.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** every like, comment and share comes from a person who chose to do it.
- **Fail:** software or an agreement produces it.

### The fix

Cancel the tool and leave the group. Post less often and answer comments yourself.

### Source

- [LinkedIn User Agreement, section 8.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement):
  > Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages, create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts, or otherwise drive inauthentic engagement;
- [LinkedIn Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies):
  > Don't do things to artificially increase engagement with your content.

## Check 24: Shared logins and copied cookies

**Priority:** Fix first
**Based on:** LinkedIn rule

### What to look at

Anywhere a LinkedIn password or session cookie has been handed over: a cloud outreach tool, an assistant, an agency, or a shared password manager entry.

### How to check it

Ask each user: has anyone else, or any tool, ever had your LinkedIn login or session cookie? Look at the active sessions in your LinkedIn account settings for devices you do not recognise.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** only the account owner has ever signed in to each account.
- **Fail:** any person or tool holds the password or a copied cookie.

### The fix

Disconnect the tool, sign out of other sessions and change the password. Never paste a LinkedIn cookie into any tool again, this kit included.

### Source

- [LinkedIn User Agreement, section 8.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement):
  > Create a false identity on LinkedIn, misrepresent your identity, create a Member profile for anyone other than yourself (a real person), or use or attempt to use another’s account (such as sharing log-in credentials or copying cookies);

## Check 25: AI personas and fake profiles

**Priority:** Fix first
**Based on:** LinkedIn rule

### What to look at

Any profile that is not one real person: a named AI SDR with its own profile, a made up salesperson, a profile with a generated photo, or staff listed at a company they do not work for.

### How to check it

List every profile used for outreach, and name the real person behind each. Check that the name, photo and job on the profile are that person's.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** every profile is one real, named person who really works there.
- **Fail:** any profile is a persona, a generated person or a false job.

### The fix

Remove the persona from LinkedIn. Your AI agent can have a name in your own product. It should not have a LinkedIn profile.

### Source

- [LinkedIn Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies):
  > We don’t allow fake profiles or entities.
  > Do not associate yourself on LinkedIn with a business or organization that you are not actually professionally associated with.

## Check 26: One person, one account

**Priority:** Fix first
**Based on:** LinkedIn rule

### What to look at

Second accounts, rented or bought accounts, accounts borrowed from staff or friends, and accounts opened after an earlier one was restricted.

### How to check it

Ask each person how many accounts they control. Look in your bills for account rental services.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** each person uses only their own single account, and no account was opened to get around a restriction.
- **Fail:** any account is rented, borrowed, duplicated or a workaround.

### The fix

Stop using those accounts. If you are already restricted, go to [the restriction guide](../guides/IF-YOUR-ACCOUNT-IS-RESTRICTED.md) instead of opening another one.

### Source

- [LinkedIn User Agreement, section 2.1](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement):
  > you will only have one LinkedIn account, which must be in your real name; and (3) you are not already restricted by LinkedIn from using the Services.
- [LinkedIn User Agreement, section 2.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement):
  > not share or transfer your account or any part of it
- [LinkedIn Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies):
  > And do not share your LinkedIn account with anyone else.

## Check 27: Working around limits

**Priority:** Fix first
**Based on:** LinkedIn rule

### What to look at

Anything built to get more than LinkedIn gives you: rotating accounts, proxies used to look like someone else, slowing software down so it passes as human, or spreading sends across staff to stay under a limit.

### How to check it

Read your tool settings for limit, proxy, delay and warm up features. Ask what they are for. A feature that exists to avoid detection is the answer.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** when you hit a limit, you stop and wait.
- **Fail:** anything exists to go around, hide from or spread out a limit.

### The fix

Turn those features off. Do not replace them with a daily number you found online, because LinkedIn does not publish one. A vendor's safe number is only that vendor's claim.

### Source

- [LinkedIn User Agreement, section 8.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement):
  > Override any security feature or bypass or circumvent any access controls or use limits of the Services (such as search results, profiles, or videos);
- [LinkedIn Help, commercial use limit](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a564226):
  > We are not able to display the exact number of searches or views you have left and we also cannot lift the limit upon request.
- [LinkedIn Help, invitation limit](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555):
  > All LinkedIn members, including those with Basic and Premium accounts, are subject to these limits.

## Check 28: Invitations people actually want

**Priority:** Fix soon
**Based on:** LinkedIn rule

### What to look at

Who you invite and what you say. Invitations used as a sales pitch, sent to people you have no reason to contact, and your pile of ignored or pending invitations.

### How to check it

Read your last batch of invitations as if you received them. Open your sent invitations and look at how many are still pending.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** invitations go to people with a clear reason to connect, and none carry a sales pitch.
- **Fail:** invitations carry promotional messages, or most sit ignored.

### The fix

Send fewer and more specific invitations, written by the person sending them. Have a person read anything AI drafted before it goes out.

### Source

- [LinkedIn Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies):
  > Do not use our invitation feature to send promotional messages to people you don't know or to otherwise spam people.
- [LinkedIn Help, types of restrictions for sending invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012/types-of-restrictions-for-sending-invitations):
  > Many of your invitations have been ignored, left pending, or marked as spam by the recipients.
  > If you send an excessive number of invitations and we suspect the use of an automation tool, we may suspend or restrict your account.

## Check 29: What your own product does on LinkedIn

**Priority:** Fix first
**Based on:** LinkedIn rule

### What to look at

If you sell an AI SDR, your product itself. Does it ask customers for a LinkedIn login or cookie, drive a browser, or take actions on LinkedIn for them? The agreement covers developing and supporting such software, not only using it.

### How to check it

Read your own onboarding flow and code. Search your repository for the word with `python3 tools/scan_site.py path/to/your/code --fail-on medium`, then read what each match does.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** your product takes no action on LinkedIn, or only what a written agreement with LinkedIn allows.
- **Fail:** it scrapes, automates or collects LinkedIn logins, or you cannot say for sure.

### The fix

Move the product's channel to ones you control, such as email to properly sourced contacts, or draft only features where a person sends the message. Get legal advice before you change what you have promised customers.

### Source

- [LinkedIn User Agreement, section 8.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement):
  > Develop, support or use software, devices, scripts, robots or any other means or processes (such as crawlers, browser plugins and add-ons or any other technology) to scrape or copy the Services, including profiles and other data from the Services;
- [LinkedIn User Agreement, section 3.4](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement):
  > LinkedIn reserves the right to restrict, suspend, or terminate your account if you breach this Contract or the law or are misusing the Services

## Check 30: Cold email rules when your agents leave LinkedIn

**Priority:** Fix soon
**Based on:** Law

### What to look at

Your outbound email, because moving away from LinkedIn moves the risk to another channel. Sender details, postal address, opt out, subject lines, and who is responsible when an agent or an agency sends for you.

### How to check it

Open your last 3 sent campaigns. Check each one against the FTC guide below for US recipients, and ask a lawyer about other countries. Test the opt out link yourself.

### Pass or fail test

- **Pass:** every campaign has honest sender details, a real postal address, a working opt out, and honest subject lines.
- **Fail:** any campaign is missing one of them.

### The fix

Fix the template so that every campaign inherits these, and name one person who owns it.

### Source

- [FTC, CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business):
  > The law makes no exception for business-to-business email.
  > you can’t contract away your legal responsibility to comply with the law.
- [LinkedIn User Agreement, section 8.1](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement):
  > Comply with all applicable laws, including, without limitation, privacy laws, intellectual property laws, anti-spam laws, export control laws, laws governing the content shared, and other applicable laws and regulatory requirements;
