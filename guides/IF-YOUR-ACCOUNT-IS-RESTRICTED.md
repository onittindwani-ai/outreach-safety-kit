# What to do if your account is restricted

Read this before you do anything else. The first hours are when people make it worse. Everything about LinkedIn's process below comes from LinkedIn's own help pages, read on 2026-11-09 and listed in [SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md). LinkedIn changes these pages, and what you see on your own screen wins. This is not legal advice.

## In the first hour

1. **Do not open a new account.** The User Agreement says you agree you are "not already restricted by LinkedIn from using the Services".
2. **Do not ask a teammate to log in for you,** and do not move the work to someone else's account.
3. **Save the evidence.** Screenshot the notice, save any email, and write down the date and what you were doing.
4. **Stop every tool that touches LinkedIn.** Do this before you contact anyone.
5. **Write down the facts** using the sheet at the end of this page.
6. **Tell your team and your customers only what you know.** Do not guess the reason. LinkedIn Support may not tell you.

## Work out which kind it is

LinkedIn's account restrictions page lists four kinds of reason: content, profile, identity and automated tools violations. Read your notice for which applies.

| What you see | What LinkedIn's help pages say | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A limit on sending invitations | "This restriction typically lasts one week." and it cannot be shortened on request | Wait. Then use check 28 before you invite anyone else |
| A suspension for automated activity | Disable the software or extension, and the account "will then automatically be re-enabled at the time specified on the suspension notification" | Remove the tool completely. Check 21 lists how to find it |
| A prompt to verify your identity | LinkedIn says it needs to confirm "you're the rightful account owner", using its verification provider Persona | Follow the steps shown when you log in. Check that you are really on linkedin.com |
| A content or policy action | "If you believe action taken on your content or your account was in error, you can submit an appeal." | Appeal once, truthfully, with facts |
| Your search is limited | The commercial use limit resets monthly and LinkedIn "cannot lift the limit upon request" | Wait, or use a LinkedIn paid plan. Do not use a workaround |
| Your company page and staff profiles have gone | See "If it hits the whole company" below | Follow that section |

**Repeat offences are worse.** LinkedIn's help page says "Repeated suspensions may result in permanent restriction of your LinkedIn account."

## Before you write to LinkedIn

Run this kit first, so what you say is true. The order that mirrors the Artisan account given to TechCrunch:

1. Run group 1. Remove LinkedIn's name from your website where you can.
2. Run group 2. Send the [questionnaire](VENDOR-QUESTIONNAIRE.md) to your data vendors, and stop using any vendor that does not answer clearly.
3. Run group 3. Disable automation.
4. Score the audit, and keep the report as your record.

## Writing to LinkedIn

Keep it short and true. LinkedIn's agreement forbids misusing the appeals process, including "duplicative, fraudulent or unfounded" appeals. Send one. Claiming you did nothing wrong when you were running an automation tool is the kind of thing that can make it worse.

You write and send this yourself, through the route shown on your notice or in the reply you received. This draft is a starting point:

```text
Hello,

My account [PROFILE LINK] was restricted on [DATE]. I want to resolve this and
follow your terms.

What I have done since the notice:
1. [Removed tool X from every device on DATE] [delete if not true]
2. [Removed mentions of LinkedIn from our website on DATE] [delete if not true]
3. [Stopped using data from vendor Y on DATE, pending its written answers] [delete if not true]

Please tell me if there is anything else you need from me.

[YOUR REAL NAME]
```

Delete every line that is not true. Do not add anything you have not done.

## If it hits the whole company

LinkedIn's agreement lets it restrict accounts for breaches, and its Brand Guidelines say misuse can lead to suspension or termination of an account. Artisan's CEO described the team restricted as a whole, with the company page, employee profiles and executive posts gone. See [what happened to Artisan](WHAT-HAPPENED-TO-ARTISAN.md).

1. Name one person to handle it, and have everyone else stop.
2. Use the contact route LinkedIn gave you. In Artisan's case, the CEO said the team was reachable only by email.
3. Do the fixes first, then reply with what you actually changed.
4. Decide who else needs to hear from you: customers first, then investors.
5. Tell sales to use the channels you own while this is open. Check 30 covers email rules.

There is no promised timeline. Artisan's CEO reported about 2 weeks in its case. That is one company's experience and may not match yours.

## Prepare now, before it happens

- Keep your customer list and pipeline in your own systems, not only as LinkedIn connections.
- Build an audience you can reach without LinkedIn, such as an email list of people who opted in.
- Keep your audit report from this kit, with dates. It shows what you did and when.
- Make sure more than one person knows where your company page admin rights sit.
