# Website wording fix guide

Before and after examples for the problems the scanner finds. Every example is synthetic: the company, the numbers and the claims are made up. Replace them with claims you can prove.

## The order to work in

1. **Delete it if you can.** Artisan's fix, per its CEO, was to remove all mentions from its site. Nothing in LinkedIn's guidelines requires you to mention LinkedIn.
2. **Describe the job instead of the company.** Say what you do for the customer.
3. **If you truly must name it**, for example to describe a service you really offer, use plain characters and the ® symbol, make it small, and add a line saying you are separate. LinkedIn's page allows this "to describe the services that you offer". Whether a given sentence fits is LinkedIn's call and your lawyer's, not ours.

Never replace the word with a wink such as "the big professional network" while still pointing at the same data or the same automation. If the product or the data is the problem, fixing the sentence does not fix it.

## Comparisons (check 1)

### Example 1: Fresher than

Before: "Fresher contacts than LinkedIn."

After: "Every contact is checked every 30 days."

Why: names no one, and states what you do. Only say it if you do it.

### Example 2: Feature table row

Before: a pricing table column headed "LinkedIn" against a column headed "ExampleCo".

After: columns headed "Manual research" and "ExampleCo".

Why: compares with the old way of working instead of a company.

### Example 3: Alternative page

Before: "The best LinkedIn alternative for finding buyers."

After: "Find buyers in your target market, with the source shown for every contact."

Why: removes the name and leads with something a buyer can check.

### Example 4: Accuracy claim

Before: "More accurate job titles than LinkedIn."

After: "Job titles confirmed against the company's own website."

Why: says how you check it, which is stronger than a comparison.

## Domains and addresses (checks 2 and 3)

### Example 5: Page address

Before: `/linkedin-outreach`

After: `/social-outreach`

Why: LinkedIn's agreement restricts its name in a URL. Redirect the old address.

### Example 6: Product name

Before: "LinkedBoost AI"

After: "ExampleCo Reach"

Why: a name built to sound like LinkedIn can be confusingly similar.

### Example 7: Shared inbox

Before: `linkedin@example.com`

After: `outreach@example.com`

Why: the agreement restricts the name in any email address.

### Example 8: Page title

Before: "The LinkedIn AI for sales teams | ExampleCo"

After: "AI sales assistant for small teams | ExampleCo"

Why: keeps the name out of the most prominent place on the page.

## Logos (check 4)

### Example 9: Logo wall

Before: an "Integrates with" row that includes the LinkedIn Logo.

After: an "Integrates with" row showing only companies whose logo terms you have followed, with LinkedIn left out.

Why: LinkedIn does not allow its full logo without a licence.

### Example 10: Footer icon

Before: a large [in] Logo in a hero banner.

After: a small [in] Logo in the footer, linking to your own company page.

Why: a link to your own page is one of the listed allowed uses.

## Partner and endorsement claims (check 5)

### Example 11: Partner line

Before: "Official LinkedIn partner."

After: delete the line.

Why: only true with LinkedIn's written consent, and then use the exact wording it gave you.

### Example 12: Integration line

Before: "Works natively with LinkedIn."

After: "Works with your CRM and your inbox."

Why: "works with" implies a relationship you may not have. Name what you really connect to.

### Example 13: Approved badge

Before: a badge reading "LinkedIn Approved".

After: remove the badge.

Why: no approval exists for you to show.

### Example 14: Founder bio

Before: "Certified LinkedIn growth expert."

After: "Ran outbound sales for small software teams for 6 years."

Why: LinkedIn's agreement gives "an accredited LinkedIn trainer" as its own example of what not to imply.

## Data claims (check 6)

### Example 15: Where data comes from

Before: "Powered by LinkedIn data."

After: "Contacts come from licensed data suppliers. Ask us for the list."

Why: only say the after version if it is true and you can show it. See group 2.

### Example 16: Enrichment

Before: "Enrich every lead from LinkedIn profiles."

After: "Add company size, industry and role from your licensed sources."

Why: removes the claim. If you really do this, change the product first.

### Example 17: Import feature

Before: "Export your Sales Navigator lists straight into ExampleCo."

After: "Import a CSV of contacts you have the right to use."

Why: Sales Navigator is a LinkedIn mark, and exporting from it points at copying data.

### Example 18: Search result copy

Before: meta description reading "LinkedIn scraper for SDR teams."

After: meta description reading "AI sales assistant that drafts follow ups for a person to approve."

Why: hidden text counts. Search engines and competitors read it.

## Automation promises (check 7)

### Example 19: Undetectable

Before: "Undetectable LinkedIn automation."

After: delete the line, then see checks 22 and 27.

Why: this promises to get around LinkedIn's systems.

### Example 20: Safe limits

Before: "Stay under LinkedIn's limits automatically, so you never get banned."

After: "Your AI writes the drafts. A person reads and sends each one."

Why: nobody outside LinkedIn knows the limits, and no one can promise "never".

### Example 21: Hands free

Before: "Connect and message prospects on LinkedIn on autopilot."

After: "Drafts your follow ups so your team can send more thoughtful messages."

Why: the before version describes what the agreement forbids.

## Disclaimers and hidden copy (checks 5 and 10)

### Example 22: Missing separation line

Before: "We help consultants improve their LinkedIn profiles." and nothing else.

After: "We help consultants improve their LinkedIn® profiles. We are an independent company, not affiliated with or endorsed by LinkedIn Corporation."

Why: describes a real service in plain characters and makes the separation clear.

### Example 23: Image alt text

Before: `alt="Our LinkedIn lead list dashboard"`

After: `alt="ExampleCo contact list dashboard, sample data"`

Why: alt text is hidden from most visitors, but not from a search.

### Example 24: Testimonial

Before: "ExampleCo got me 40 replies on LinkedIn in a week, without lifting a finger."

After: "ExampleCo's drafts saved me hours every week." (Only with that customer's permission and only if true.)

Why: a customer quote on your site is your claim too. Check it the same way.
